Stop abstaining when we are holding the payer's own public policy

The pilot put ChatGPT and OrthoAppeals on the same 60 denial letters. On six
UnitedHealthcare letters ChatGPT returned surgery-knee.pdf, surgery-shoulder.pdf
and spinal-fusion-decompression.pdf -- real, current, public UHC policies that
name the denied code -- and OrthoAppeals told the patient no criteria exist.
The scorer recorded ChatGPT hallucinating. It had not. We had the document, its
URL sitting in the row, and we handed the patient nothing.

The research was not wrong: those policies do send medical-necessity criteria
to InterQual. The vocabulary was wrong. "NO PUBLIC CRITERIA" was doing two jobs
-- the payer publishes nothing at all, and the payer publishes the policy but
not the criteria -- and the app treated both as silence.

  - New status DOCUMENT PUBLIC, CRITERIA VENDOR-HELD, and code D in the
    coverage build. 896 rows, 13% of the directory, move from telling the
    patient nothing to handing them their plan's policy plus the route to
    demand the criteria. A row qualifies only if the document's own audience
    matches the row's plan type.
  - 368 UnitedHealthcare commercial rows were pointed at UHC's MEDICARE
    ADVANTAGE policies (MMP052.13, MMP089.18) by the 2026-08-26 catalog sweep.
    Serving an MA document to a commercial member is the defect we already
    fixed once for HCSC portals. Repointed from the Oxford rows, which still
    carried the correct commercial mapping.
  - Third study stratum, vendor_held, with gold behavior cite_and_route.
    Correct requires both halves: the document alone leaves the patient holding
    a policy that lacks the standard they were judged against; the route alone
    withholds a document we have.

The migration is scripted, not hand-edited: scripts/policy_platform/
fix_vendor_held_docs.py, with the pre-change CSV kept beside it.

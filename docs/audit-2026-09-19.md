# End-to-end audit of the pipeline, 2026-09-19

Prompted by: "some things could be fooling us." Three things were.

---

## 1. OrthoAppeals' 60/60 is circular. It is not a result.

`build_cases.refresh_gold()` builds the answer key by reading
`data/policy_platform/app_option_policy_directory.csv` and writing
`gold.policy_url = row["policy_url"]`.

`retrieve.run_ortho()` answers by reading the same CSV, finding the same row,
and returning `answer.policy_url = row["policy_url"]`.

Same field. Same row. Same file. Checked across the arm: **the OrthoAppeals URL
equals the gold URL byte for byte in 60 of 60 cases.** That arm cannot be scored
wrong. Its 100% is a tautology.

It compounds: the 60 cases were *sampled from that CSV*, and the three strata are
its own `status` column. So library coverage is 100% by sampling design too. A
patient whose payer is not in the library is a case this study cannot currently
generate.

### What the three chatbot arms still measure

They had to find the document from scratch with a search engine and a fetcher,
so ChatGPT 38/60, and the Gemini and Claude-free numbers, are real — **provided
the key is true**, which is what section 2 is about.

### What OrthoAppeals can honestly claim

Not "it finds the governing policy better than a chatbot" — it does not find
anything, it looks up a curated library. The defensible claim is narrower and
still worth making:

> A curated, independently verified policy library returns the governing
> document where a general chatbot with web search largely does not.

The measurable risks for that arm are **coverage** (does the library hold this
payer/procedure at all) and **staleness** (is the URL still live and still
governing), not retrieval skill. Coverage cannot be measured while cases are
sampled from the library itself; the main study needs a sampling frame drawn
independently of the directory.

## 2. Nothing had ever checked the key against the live documents

Until now "correct" meant "matches our CSV". `scripts/study/verify_gold.py`
fetches every document the key names and asks whether it exists and governs the
denied code. First run:

| stratum | result |
|---|---|
| in_library (30) | **29 verified** — resolves, names the procedure, states criteria. 1 flagged. |
| vendor_held (15) | 5 shaped as expected; **10 flagged** (see §3) |
| no_policy (15) | unverifiable by fetch, by definition |

`poc-1acf427871` (Fidelis, CPT 27446 unicompartmental knee) points at the 2026
Evolent Musculoskeletal Surgery guideline. The cached copy is 120k characters
and contains "unicompartmental" nowhere; a live refetch returned nothing.
Needs a human look before it scores any arm.

**Stratum C's key is not independently verifiable.** The claim is that the payer
publishes nothing, and no fetch can confirm a negative. Those 15 rest on our
research note, and the write-up has to say so rather than presenting all three
strata as equally grounded.

## 3. A quote can be verbatim, from the right document, and still be the wrong sentence

`quote_check` confirms a quotation appears in the policy text. Nothing confirms
it is a **coverage criterion**. `find_criteria()` feeds
`appeal_letter._ground_citations()`, so whatever it selects is what a patient
sends their insurer. Reading real OrthoAppeals letters:

- `poc-1c4d453f27` quotes *"Coverage Rationale Surgery of the hip ... is proven
  and medically necessary in certain circumstances."* — a section header. It
  states no rule; the circumstances are precisely what it does not say.
- The same letter quotes *"Arthroscopy, Diagnostic, +/- Synovial Biopsy, Hip
  Arthroscopy, Surgical, Hip ..."* — a row out of the CPT coding table.
- `poc-177b09d7a6` quotes *"Surgery may be an option for individuals whose pain
  cannot be controlled by more conservative methods (National Institute of
  Arthritis and Musculoskeletal and Skin Diseases ...)"* — the policy's
  background literature, not the plan's criteria.
- Several letters quote *"Medical records documentation may be required ... but
  does not guarantee coverage"* — administrative boilerplate.
- Worst: `poc-1c4d453f27` quotes *"Surgical treatment for Femoroacetabular
  Impingement Syndrome is **unproven and not medically necessary** in the
  presence of advanced osteoarthritis (Tönnis Grade ...)"*. That is the
  exclusion. In an appeal it hands the insurer its own denial rationale.
- `poc-87e365caed` produced a letter with **no quotations at all**.

The ten `vendor_but_criteria_found` flags in §2 are the same defect seen from
the other end: all ten are UnitedHealthcare or Molina policies the key calls
vendor-held, and the "criteria" extracted from them are these headers. The
stratum label is right; the extractor is manufacturing criteria.

### What this does to the abstract

> "OrthoAppeals quoted the policy 155 times, 4 of them inaccurately (3%)"

That sentence is true and measures the wrong thing. 3% misquotation is
irrelevant if a meaningful share of the 97% are headers, code tables, background
prose, or the exclusion that sinks the appeal. **This claim must not be
published as it stands.**

A rough pass over the 98 quoted lines in the 60 OrthoAppeals letters puts at
least 12–15% in the demonstrably-not-a-criterion classes, with a further ~37%
needing a read. The regex that produced those buckets is itself noisy — it
flagged "NSAID or acetaminophen >= three weeks unless contraindicated", a
perfectly good conservative-care criterion, as adverse — so the number to
publish has to come from a human-reviewable pass, not from that heuristic.

---

## What has to happen before any of this is written up

1. `find_criteria()` must reject headers, coding tables, administrative
   boilerplate and background/literature prose, and must never select an
   exclusion without the letter rebutting it. This is a **product** defect:
   patients are sending these letters.
2. The relevance of a quotation needs a check of its own, alongside the
   existing presence check.
3. The primary endpoint must be reframed as library coverage + accuracy vs.
   retrieval, and the OrthoAppeals arm reported against the independently
   verified key only.
4. Decide, with Kassam: the 11 flagged key entries get fixed or dropped.
5. The main study needs a sampling frame independent of the directory, or it
   cannot say anything about coverage.
